# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Independent Chinese content creators who write once and publish across image-card platforms, WeChat articles, and personal knowledge systems. They work on desktop and iPad, often moving between Markdown, images, Live Photos, Obsidian, and final export while staying focused on the current draft.

## Product Purpose

写了就发 turns one source draft into paginated graphic cards or a structured long-form article in the same workspace. Success means a creator can write, format, preview every output, and export without rebuilding the layout in another tool or losing their place in the editor.

## Brand Personality

Restrained, dependable, creator-focused. The interface should feel mature and calm, with direct language and familiar editing behavior.

## Anti-references

- Desktop-only interfaces that merely shrink on touch devices.
- All-black or beige paper-like themes that overpower the content.
- Decorative SaaS dashboards, glass effects, gradients, and promotional clutter inside the working surface.
- Floating controls, hidden state changes, or automatic actions that remove the user's ability to decide.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the creator in context: formatting, previewing, and saving must not move the editor or lose the selection.
2. Make state and consequences explicit: saving, syncing, exporting, and destructive actions require visible feedback and user control.
3. Adapt structure to the device: desktop supports simultaneous panels; iPad prioritizes touch, safe areas, drawers, and deliberate preview modes.
4. Preserve one source of truth: card and long-form outputs share the same content and the same codebase across local and online modes.
5. Protect trust boundaries: distinguish local storage, cloud sync, generated files, and published results without implying guarantees that were not verified.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use WCAG AA contrast as the baseline. Support keyboard and touch input, visible focus, reduced motion, iPad safe areas, 44 px touch targets on coarse pointers, and status communication that does not rely on color alone.
